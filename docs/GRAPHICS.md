# Graphics status

## Status

The trainer graphics are in progress.

The current SVG system proves layout, customization, articulation, access
adaptation, layering, directionality, and repeating demonstrations. It is not
production-finished illustration and must not be treated as authoritative
movement instruction.

The Care route adds a separate procedural Three.js shoe reference. It has
distinct toe, heel, side, flex, seam/welt, and sole geometry; neutral-black
dielectric material ownership; a local calibration hall; and explicit
hand/tool contact motion. It is presented as unrated and unverified, not as a
replacement for the trainer's legacy shoe strokes or as production footwear.

## Implemented foundation

- front, profile, seated, and grounded floor configurations;
- separate upper-arm, forearm, upper-leg, shin, foot, head, and body tracks;
- visible elbow and knee joints;
- directional walking and profile-facing logic;
- clothing, covering, hair, accessory, and support layers;
- height and weight scaling;
- paper/fiber marks, film grain, subtle weave, and softened edges;
- theme-stable facial colors;
- movement-specific phase data and reduced-motion poses.

## Known gaps

- Some named poses still require closer visual alignment with their written
  instruction.
- The two-dimensional rig cannot yet express every rotation cleanly.
- Seated and profile anatomy require further redraws.
- Hand, foot, mouth, gaze, and weight direction require movement-by-movement
  review.
- Clothing and coverings require exhaustive occlusion testing across hair,
  body shape, view, support, and movement.
- Secondary motion must better express material weight without obscuring joint
  paths.
- Natural-fiber texture requires more visible, consistent treatment without
  increasing blur.
- The movement catalog requires review by qualified practitioners.
- No authored footwear mesh, macro/micro texture set, or resource manifest is
  present.
- The reference renderer does not implement the required separate raw-mip-0,
  PMREM, SSR/confidence, box-projection, finite-source-glint, and atomic
  two-shoe capture pipeline.
- Full-avatar, compact, peer, seated, profile, fallback, and other shoe
  consumers still use legacy two-dimensional strokes.
- No measured physical contact, production optical measurement, device,
  performance, or independent visual evidence has been admitted. The local
  fail-closed capability sentinel is not optical-conformance evidence.

## Quality gates

A movement is ready only when:

1. its name, starting position, written directions, and animation agree;
2. the trainer uses the correct standing, seated, or floor relationship;
3. facing, gaze, mouth, hands, feet, and weight transfer make spatial sense;
4. elbows and knees bend at visible, attached joints;
5. supports contact the body and floor plausibly;
6. clothing remains correctly layered and its lag reflects material weight;
7. front, profile, reduced-motion, light, and dark presentations pass review;
8. the form is compatible with the selected access setup;
9. an informed reviewer confirms that the simplified sequence remains
   recognizable.

## Design direction

The target is a tactile, contemplative cut-paper character system with large,
expressive eyes; clear profile silhouettes; restrained squash and stretch;
visible weight transfer; and clothing that follows rather than detaches from
the body.

Softness must come from palette, texture, timing, and shape—not fuzzy rendering
or ambiguous anatomy.
