# UX and UI

## Information architecture

Evenward exposes five primary areas:

- **Today:** choose a direction and begin a short thread.
- **Practice:** browse activities directly.
- **Chess:** play a saved, unclocked game.
- **Patterns:** inspect and clear the data boundary.
- **Learn:** browse movement disciplines and activate one repeating trainer
  demonstration.

The home question asks which direction feels closest. It does not ask what a
person needs, infer a condition, or recommend a correct state.

## Progressive disclosure

- The first screen presents only directional choices and one suggested thread.
- Practice detail appears after selection.
- Learn categories begin closed.
- Only one lesson opens and commands the trainer at a time.
- Advanced appearance and access settings live in modal panels.
- Explanations are short at the point of action, with deeper literacy behind
  disclosures.

This preserves conceptual depth without turning every screen into a dashboard.

## Language standard

Use:

- “slow things down” and “downregulating direction”;
- “invite some energy” and “upregulating direction”;
- “gather attention,” “move,” and “observe”;
- “may,” “can explore,” and “choose”;
- literal movement directions and evidence boundaries.

Avoid:

- claims that the application knows a person’s state;
- language that labels one direction as correct;
- promises of reset, healing, treatment, or guaranteed calm;
- completion pressure, moralized streaks, and urgency;
- language that turns observations into diagnoses.

Daypart wording comes from the browser clock and is never inferred from theme.

## Trainer behavior

The full-size trainer remains visually central:

- right-aligned on large screens;
- prominent above or below core content on mobile;
- continually alive through restrained idle movement;
- responsive to permitted interactions;
- facing the direction of travel;
- repeating the selected movement until Next, Stop, or a replacement command.

When demonstrating, the trainer—not a mini preview—owns the movement. Text
names the movement, gives a literal starting position, and explains the ordered
action concisely.

Movement setup, support, clothing, and movement requirements determine which
form is presented. The system does not infer personal capacity.

## Visual language

- Jost is the sole interface typeface.
- Forest, sea, and sunrise each offer light and dark variants.
- Glassmorphism provides hierarchy through translucent surfaces, visible
  borders, and backdrop blur.
- Restrained skeuomorphism appears through inset edges, material cues, stone
  chess pieces, paper/fiber texture, and gentle depth.
- Rounded corners and low-saturation palettes support softness without reducing
  contrast.
- Ambient rain, leaves, waves, clouds, and stars are dense but slow, decorative,
  and non-interactive.

More-opaque surfaces and reduced motion are first-class alternatives, not
afterthoughts.

## Interaction principles

- Native controls before custom interaction.
- One clear primary action per region.
- Reversible choices and visible deletion.
- No hidden capture on Patterns.
- No penalty for pausing, repetition, changing direction, or leaving.
- Personality details never block task completion.

## Current UX/UI limitations

The interface structure is substantially more mature than the trainer art.
Movement graphics, seated/profile geometry, support contact, garment occlusion,
and secondary motion require further design and validation. See
[Graphics status](GRAPHICS.md).
